# Community, reputation, and fraud controls

Reviews are subjective community records. They do not become objective evidence merely because they are popular.

- Reviews retain author, subject, rating, use case, verified-use signal, status, content hash, and the reputation weight used at publication.
- Helpfulness votes are unique per user and review. Weight ranges from 1 to 3, preventing high-reputation accounts from dominating.
- Suggested edits preserve their proposed patch, rationale, submitter, reviewer, decision, and timestamps.
- Reports become moderation cases with explicit investigation, action, dismissal, appeal, and resolution states.
- Reputation is an append-only ledger of explainable events. Penalties are explicit events rather than silent score changes.
- Initial fraud heuristics flag normalized duplicate content and concentrated review bursts. Findings enter moderation; they do not autonomously punish users.

Future graph and behavioral signals must follow the same principles: bounded influence, human review, appeal, privacy minimization, and an explanation retained with every action.
